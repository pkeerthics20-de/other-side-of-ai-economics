The Other Side of the AI Economy:
Who Actually Captures the Value?

AI is increasing productivity. AI companies are generating revenue. Investment is exploding. But there is another question we don't discuss enough: Who actually captures the economic value created by AI?

By Keerthi • Data Engineer • Technology & AI Enthusiast

🤔 The question after the question

In my previous article, I looked at AI from the perspective of human work. The conclusion was simple:

AI may not replace entire teams overnight. It may reduce the amount of human effort required to deliver the same outcome.

But that creates a second economic question. If AI makes a company more productive... where does the economic value created by that productivity actually go?

💰 First: Look at the money going into AI

The scale of investment is extraordinary. According to Stanford's 2026 AI Index, global corporate AI investment reached approximately $581.7 billion in 2025. That was more than double the previous year.

📈 But investment isn't the same as revenue

AI companies are also generating revenue at extraordinary speed. OpenAI reported approximately $13 billion of revenue in 2025. But Reuters also reported approximately $8 billion of expenditure for the year.

And this is where the economics become interesting. Revenue is not profit. Investment is not revenue. And expenditure is not automatically loss. We need to be careful not to manufacture a "profit" number when the company does not publicly disclose an AI-specific profit figure.

The important distinction

Productivity ≠ Revenue
Revenue ≠ Profit
Investment ≠ Return

The question is not whether AI creates value.

The question is:

Who captures that value?

📊 Productivity does not automatically mean more revenue

Consider a simple example. A company spends ₹1 crore to operate a process. AI reduces the cost to ₹60 lakh. That's a significant productivity improvement. But suppose revenue remains ₹1.2 crore. The company didn't suddenly double its revenue. It simply improved the economics of delivering the same output.

The paradox

AI can become more efficient per task...

while total AI demand continues to grow.

Efficiency gains can reduce the resources required per task, but greater adoption and more energy-intensive applications can increase total demand.

🧱 Could physical constraints create a new ceiling?

I don't think the correct argument is: "AI will run out of chips or water." That's too simplistic. A better question is: "How quickly can physical infrastructure scale compared with the demand for AI?"

Software can scale extremely quickly. Physical infrastructure cannot.

A model can be improved through software. But building:

  • new semiconductor fabs
  • new data centers
  • new transmission capacity
  • new power generation
  • new cooling infrastructure
  • new networking infrastructure

requires capital, materials, energy, construction time, regulatory approvals and skilled labour. The IEA specifically identifies bottlenecks across electricity infrastructure, transformers, advanced chips and other components as constraints on near-term data-center expansion.

So I wouldn't predict an "AI resource depletion collapse". I'd call it something more realistic: the economics of scaling AI may become increasingly dependent on physical infrastructure.

But the loop doesn't end there. Business value creates demand for more AI. More AI creates demand for more compute. More compute requires more infrastructure. More infrastructure requires more capital. And the economic value gets distributed across the ecosystem.

🎯 My Perspective

I'm not arguing that AI is a bubble. I'm not arguing that AI doesn't create value. And I'm definitely not arguing that AI won't improve productivity. It already is.

My question is about the economics underneath the excitement.

AI can increase productivity without automatically doubling business revenue.

A company can use AI to replace repetitive human effort. But some of that spending can move into:

AI models → Cloud → Compute → Chips → Energy → Infrastructure → Governance → Human expertise

The cost doesn't necessarily disappear. It moves.

And that's why I think the future will not simply be:

"AI replaces humans."

It will be a redistribution of economic value across humans, AI companies, cloud providers, chip manufacturers, infrastructure companies and the businesses actually using AI.

The real race is not simply to build AI.

It is to capture economic value from AI.

And for individuals, the same principle applies. Don't just learn how to use AI. Combine:

AI + Technology + Business + Domain Expertise

Because the person who understands the business problem, knows the domain, knows how to use AI and can measure the economic outcome may become much more valuable than someone who only knows how to perform the underlying task.

Productivity is the beginning of the AI story. Value capture is the bigger story.
